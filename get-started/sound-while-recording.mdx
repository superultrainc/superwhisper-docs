---
title: "Sound while recording"
description: "Choose what happens to your music and videos while you dictate: keep playing, pause, mute, lower, or muffle."
---

Open **Settings → Sound** and pick an option for **Sound while recording**. Superwhisper puts your audio back as it was when the recording ends.

| Option | What happens to your audio |
|---|---|
| **Keep Playing** | Keeps playing at full volume. |
| **Pause** | Pauses, and resumes when you stop recording. |
| **Mute** | Goes silent while you record. |
| **Lower** | Gets quieter while you record. |
| **Muffle** | Keeps just the beat and bass under your voice. |

This is the default for every mode, and it starts on **Pause**. To use a different option in one mode, change **Sound while recording** in that mode's settings. The first choice there follows this default, and is marked **(Default)**. See [Modes](/modes/modes).

## Muffle

Muffle makes your music sound like it's coming through a wall, like the party next door or the club from the bathroom. The beat and bass stay, while the vocals drop out, leaving room for your voice.

- It needs macOS 14.2 or later. On older versions the option isn't shown.
- The first time you choose it, macOS asks for the **System Audio Recording** permission. If the permission is turned off, Superwhisper switches the option to **Lower** and opens System Settings so you can turn it on. Then choose Muffle again.
- Under the hood, it turns other apps' audio down and runs it through a low-pass filter, which keeps the low end and cuts the highs.
- Some apps that reroute your Mac's audio, such as FineTune and SoundSource, need a quick setup to work with Muffle, which Superwhisper can do for you in one click. A few can't run alongside it, and Superwhisper uses **Lower** instead. See [Muffle and other audio apps](/common-issues/muffle-audio-apps).
